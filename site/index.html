What this page is, and what came before it

Waterline began as a plan for the Gemini 4 Argon hackathon in San Francisco on October 25, 2026. The build-day demo was written beforehand to guide a day of building with Google Antigravity and Gemini 4 Argon, and the thing to be built that day was this city-manager site. The build day didn't happen for Waterline, so the demo's descriptions of what Argon agents did are a plan, not results.

This page is the finished city-manager site, built afterwards with Claude Code. No AI model runs here: every number is computed in your browser from the public data listed under Method.

Method, sources, limits

How the numbers are made

Tide. NOAA CO-OPS high/low predictions for San Francisco (9414290), 15 days. The anomaly is the mean of observed minus predicted water level over the last 30 days, fetched live. Adding it to each future high assumes the anomaly holds; this October it has run near +1 ft because of the El Niño Kelvin wave.

Rain. National Weather Service gridpoint forecast for the cell over 17th & Folsom (MTR 85,105): quantitative precipitation, split by hour and summed per Pacific day.

Low spots and drains. USGS 3DEP lidar of San Francisco, resampled to a 5 m bare-earth model. Priority-flood fill finds the street bowls; D8 flow routing finds how much ground drains to each of the city's 23,835 inlets. Drains are scored by the depth of the nearest bowl (capped at 1.5 m) times the log of the area they collect. Low spots are scored by bowl volume, past 311 reports within 150 m and SFPUC 100-year zone membership, so they are not an independent check against either. The top 500 sit near 9.7 past 311 flooding reports each within 75 m, against 6.1 for all inlets. Drains in pits deeper than 2 m (underpasses, stairwells) are marked for a person to verify rather than sent to a street crew.

Limits. This is a screening model, not a hydraulic model like HEC-RAS: no pipes, no pumps, no backflow, no wind. It points at where to look first. It is not an official alert: follow AlertSF and the National Weather Service.
